2145427 Week 12 — Seven-section aircraft pulse investigation v4

1. INVESTIGATE THE SIGNAL (15 minutes)
Install Arduino IDE and UNO R4 board support before class. Extract the source ZIP,
open aircraft_pulse_source.ino, select UNO R4 Minima or WiFi and the correct port.
Enter your measured supply/reference in REFERENCE_V. Try compiling: the starter
contains one deliberate compilation error. Read the diagnostic and repair the
code yourself or with AI help, then compile again and upload.
After repair, the sketch only outputs the noisy pulse pattern at A0. It does not sample
A1 or transmit serial data. A0 uses 12-bit DAC output at 4,000 updates/s.
Connect scope CH1 tip to A0 and its ground to UNO GND. Use high-impedance input,
DC coupling and compensated 10x probes with matching scope settings.
Check the one-second cycle: 2.0 V baseline, 2.6 V long pulse at 120–320 ms,
2.5 V short pulse at 620–640 ms. Interference at three frequencies is added.
Verify actual voltage and timing. Paste your uploaded code and save an initial
copy in the worksheet. Attach a waveform screenshot with readable scales.

2. FREQUENCY SPECTRUM ANALYSIS (25 minutes)
Find out how to operate FFT and read its peaks on your GDS-1202B. This is your task.
Use the full manufacturer manual and other resources:
https://www.gwinstek.com/en-global/products/downloadSeriesDownNew/1676/130
Submit an oscilloscope FFT screenshot showing CH1, readable frequency/amplitude
axes with units, and the identified interference peaks. No FFT questionnaire or
image-detail fields are required. Keep the three frequency/amplitude rows below.
Pulse harmonics are useful content: not every peak is interference.

3. NOISE FILTER DESIGN (25 minutes)
Choose and justify low-pass or high-pass behavior. Use the worksheet equations to
calculate cutoff, R/C and expected attenuation. Predict useful-pulse changes too.
Approximately 47 kohm and non-polar 22–100 nF parts are starting stock.
For the shown unbuffered low-pass layout keep at least 33 kohm series resistance.
A different topology/lower resistance requires instructor review before powering,
including loading, bias and allowable voltage. Design answers save automatically
in the worksheet.

4. FILTER CONSTRUCTION AND VALIDATION (30 minutes)
Turn power off before changing wiring. Low-pass reference:
A0 -- R -- output node; C from output node to GND.
CH1 tip: A0 before R. CH2 tip: output node. Both grounds: UNO GND.
Do not connect another source to A0. Confirm that CH1 has not changed under load.
Record actual R and C values. Attach one scope image with both time-domain waveforms
together, then write your comparison below the screenshots. Describe changes in
ripple, pulse shape and short-pulse visibility. Support your discussion with
before/after pulse heights and widths or delays, including units and your
measurement method. Discuss the balance between smoothing and changes to useful
pulses, and note any measurement limits. A breadboard photo is optional.

5. FILTERED SIGNAL ANALYSIS (25 minutes)
Take fresh input/output FFT measurements from the assembled circuit. Attach ONE
image containing both CH1 input and CH2 RC output FFT results, with readable
channel labels, scales and units. Match acquisition/window/scale conventions.
If captured separately, combine both captures into one labelled image.
Read corresponding peaks yourself. Calculate positive attenuation for a reduction
using the typeset worksheet equations: linear RMS amplitudes require a ratio in
matching units; dBV RMS readings require subtraction. Record limitations.
Report attenuation at identified interference frequencies. A bin may also contain
pulse harmonics. A noise-floor reading is a limit, not zero output amplitude.

After the FFT measurements, return to the time-domain display. Keep CH2 connected
to the RC output and set 500 ms/div: five seconds across ten divisions. Use normal
sampling and start with a 10,000-point record. Complete an acquisition, stop it,
and export CH2 as Detail CSV containing time and voltage samples. Check the units,
approximately five seconds of data, sample interval and clipping. The first-to-last
time span may be one sample interval short of five seconds. Keep the original CSV
locally for analysis. Do not submit or attach the CSV to the worksheet.

6. DIGITAL FILTER DESIGN (45 minutes)
Use Excel, MATLAB, Python or another program to process your Section 5 CSV.
Identify the numeric time and CH2 voltage columns below any scope headers. Verify
time and voltage units, duration and uniform sampling. Check for missing/repeated
time values or clipping; re-export unsuitable data. Determine actual sample interval
Δt and sample rate 1/Δt. Do not assume 1,000 samples/s from the display scale.
Apply trailing MA and EMA independently to the same original RC voltage samples.
Use spreadsheet formulas or code; explain your work and any AI help. Initialize MA
history and EMA output with the first valid input, padding the MA history with that
value. Allow initial transients to settle before comparing. Compare MA 5/51 and
EMA 0.40/0.02, then choose your own settings. An M-sample window spans MΔt seconds;
relate its duration to the useful 20 ms short pulse. Record the program, formulas
or code, checks, settings and observations in the worksheet. No A1 wiring or new
Arduino filter sketch is needed.
Make TWO comparison figures using the same five-second recording:
1. MA: plot window lengths 5, 51 and your chosen window together.
2. EMA: plot coefficients 0.4, 0.02 and your chosen coefficient together.
Include the original RC trace as a reference in each figure. A labelled short-pulse
inset is optional. Each figure needs a descriptive title, Time (s) horizontal axis,
Voltage (V) vertical axis, and a legend identifying RC and every filter setting.
State the chosen MA window in samples or chosen EMA coefficient in the legend or
caption. Use readable text, distinguishable traces and common scales. Keep filter
delay visible rather than shifting traces to align. No trial-value table is needed.
Attach one MA image and one EMA image as PNG/JPEG/BMP/WebP (up to 10 MiB each).
Clear screenshots of the finished figures are acceptable. Submit both figures
and worksheet responses, not the CSV. Discuss how settings change smoothing,
pulse height, shape and delay and explain your choices in the observations above.
Figures alone do not establish digital-output SNR without a signal/noise method.
