SOURCE AND CONNECTIONS
A0 is the 12-bit DAC OUTPUT; A1 is the measured ADC INPUT. Both variants use
these pins. Keep common UNO/scope ground. Never connect a generator or another
active output to A0. Use compensated 10x probes and high-impedance scope inputs
(not 50 ohm loading), and
match the physical probe attenuation with the scope setting. Switch power off
before changing wiring. Verify actual voltages before relying on the ADC.

Baseline: A0 directly to A1. Both scope tips at A0; both grounds at UNO GND.
Useful tones: 50, 55, 70 Hz; 0.20 V peak each.
Interference tones: 120, 150, 180 Hz; 0.10 V peak each.
DC offset: 2.0 V. The mathematical bound is 1.1 to 2.9 V; inspect actual output.
Generation uses a 4,000-update/s hardware timer and a precomputed waveform.
A separate timer samples A1 at nominal 1,000 samples/s, with 12-bit readings.
The measured voltage reference is user-entered, not automatically calibrated.

FFT INVESTIGATION
Discover how to operate the GDS-1202B FFT using the manufacturer manual and
reliable resources, including your AI tool. Record the procedure you discover.
No FFT button or menu sequence is supplied. First establish the changing time
waveform and voltage range. Find all six peaks, including the 50/55 Hz pair.
Record source, window, record length, actual sample rate, frequency/amplitude
scales and units. Display zoom alone does not improve frequency resolution.
The native FFT bin spacing is the sample rate divided by the number of samples
actually used. Windowing broadens peaks. A settled four-second record is the
starting target; record what your instrument actually permits and obtains.
The DC offset is not one of the three useful tones.

RC DESIGN
Choose and justify the filter type, cutoff and component combination. Predict
what happens to every useful and interference tone; preserve the design first.
For the proposed low-pass topology: A0 -> your series R -> output/A1; capacitor
from output/A1 to GND. CH1 before R; CH2 at A1. Keep all probe grounds at UNO GND.
Use non-polar capacitors. Available starting stock: approximately 47 kohm and
22-100 nF. Keep at least 33 kohm in series for this unbuffered DAC arrangement.
A lower resistance or a different topology requires instructor review of loading,
bias and voltage range. A simple high-pass with a ground-referenced output can
produce negative voltages; do not energize another topology without review.
These constraints protect the source and ADC; your cutoff and R/C choices remain
yours. Record nominal/actual values, tolerances, predicted/measured tone amplitudes,
attenuation and any redesign. One RC stage gives partial suppression and also
changes useful amplitudes and phase. In dBV, subtract readings for attenuation;
do not divide logarithmic values. Missing peaks are measurement limits.

LIVE DIGITAL COMPARISON
Keep the physical RC in place. The starter's processSample initially passes the
measured voltage through; it does not claim to have MA/EMA filtering. Integrate
the working FILTER_EXAMPLES.txt definitions, replacing processSample once.
Then set DIGITAL_FILTERS to true. Examples start with MA length 5 and EMA
coefficient 0.40, with independent first-input initialization. Change parameters
only with a recorded explanation. Keep timer/source/serial routines intact.
Both digital paths consume the same A1 sample, never each other's output.
In Arduino IDE Serial Plotter choose 500,000 baud and newline commands.
Completed live traces: RC (A1), RC_MA, RC_EMA, all in volts. They are software
traces, not additional analogue output pins. Save your plot screenshot manually.
Allow startup settling. Compare useful information, interference and delay.
The horizontal axis is a sample sequence; it is not a calibrated time axis.
There is no capture, buffered recording or replay command.

DIAGNOSTICS (SEPARATE FROM PLOT LINES)
status      Pause plot lines and print measured rates, gap-based missed-slot
            estimates, interval errors, processing time, clipping and overruns.
plot        Resume the continuous live stream without restarting the waveform.
resetstats  Pause plotting and clear diagnostics; waveform/filter state continues.
help        Same diagnostic / command information as status.
Commands require newline. Use Serial Monitor for status; reopen Plotter and
send plot afterward. Reset/reconnection may restart the board. Start a clean
observation interval with resetstats then plot, wait, then status; record results.
If USB/serial cannot keep up, source, sampling and filter updates continue but
plot lines can be lost. An overrun invalidates any claim of uniformly spaced
plotted points. Resolve timing/throughput issues with the instructor.
Do not infer actual timing from baud rate. The actual scope measurement is
needed to verify source timing; software counters alone cannot establish it.
